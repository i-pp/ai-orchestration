# Editorial review

Read the title and opening without project context. Identify what the reader wants to accomplish, the obstacle, the bounded investigation, result and useful decision. A bug name alone is not a purpose. Avoid a long project introduction.

Follow the supported causal story: tools and desired work, failure, unsuccessful attempt, diagnosis, change and result. Identify an AI/task assignment when AI evaluation is the subject, not as a universal story requirement. Choose natural transitions rather than mandatory headings; explanation order must not invent discovery chronology. Distinguish resolved behavior from an unresolved part. Ask what the reader can now do or decide and what the evidence adds beyond primary documentation.

Check title promise, paragraph logic, necessary navigation and the ending together. Remove report-like lists, stock summaries and unnecessary length when they obscure the point. Do not repair weak evidence by adding prose. Keep a weak topic short or deferred. Evaluate readability independently; test counts, chronological order and first person do not establish article value.
