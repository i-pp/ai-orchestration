# Editorial policy

Apply this policy to technical articles in any medium. Read [execution policies](../docs/ai/policies.md) and [orchestration](../docs/ai/orchestration.md); this policy grants no execution or publication authority.

## Authority and loading

[Engineering Editorial Policy](../docs/ai/editorial-policy.md) is the user's authoritative editorial standard. This file supplies workflow, disclosure, review limits and human-approval rules; it does not replace that standard. Apply explicit user instructions and execution constraints first. An example illustrates editing choices, not a new factual source or an override of disclosure requirements. Report substantive conflicts rather than silently choosing one or changing the user's example.

Before authoring or review, run `python3 skills/technical-writing/scripts/load_policy.py --show` from this checkout and read the returned context. It loads the canonical standard, operational rules, Skills, roles and exact before/after samples with content hashes. See the [example calibration](../skills/article-editor/references/engineering-example.md). This working-copy context is separate from a media repository's immutable Git pin: an older pin does not include these additions. Missing canonical context is a reported hold on editorial acceptance, never a fallback to the old policy. Do not change a media pin or remote merely to make the local context appear deployed.

## Topic fitness before prose

Before treating a draft as an article, state the reader's concrete question, the evidence for resolution or non-resolution, a reproducible procedure and resulting decision, and the contribution beyond existing documentation. Negative results can be valuable if they change a specific reader decision with inspectable evidence. A sequence of events or natural first person alone is not sufficient. The Editor must answer “So what can the reader now do or decide?” independently; a Technical Reviewer's accuracy finding does not establish article value.

Choose LONG_FORM, SHORT_NOTE or DEFER with reasons. If evidence or contribution is thin, shorten or park the material; do not add invented resolution, generic lessons or length. Compare relevant primary documentation and acknowledge when the draft merely restates it. Keep missing evidence and the condition for reconsideration explicit. Record user rejection as reopening editorial acceptance; do not preserve a misleading PASS label.

## Purpose-first entry for unfamiliar readers

Write the title and opening for a reader arriving from search who knows nothing about the author or project and wants to accomplish a specific task. Before the example's chronology, state what the reader wants to achieve, the obstacle or decision, what this bounded investigation compared, and the finding or usable information. Identify the relevant model/tool/language and scope in the title plus opening. A technical symptom is evidence for that question, not a substitute for the purpose.

Connect the supported causal chain: what tools were used and what was being built, the observed failure, the attempted response that still failed, the diagnostic reasoning, the change, the measured outcome, and the specific reader takeaway. For an AI evaluation, identify which task was assigned to which AI; do not force an AI assignment into unrelated investigations. Do not turn that chain into mandatory section headings or invent missing steps. Distinguish explanation order from event chronology: do not claim a cause was discovered after a retry if earlier records already described it. Preserve failed attempts and limited results; an intention to help readers is expressed through a concrete usable distinction, not a promotional call to action or stock conclusion.

Do not repair a missing purpose with a long project introduction. Use only supported motivation; do not invent the author's experience or imply that one task proves general capability. Keep the promise aligned with the actual evidence and any unsuccessful outcome. The Editor must perform a cold-reader check on the title and opening alone: can a goal-oriented reader identify the target, problem, comparison, result and limits without learning the project first? Reopen the framing if not.

## AI writing disclosure

Disclose ChatGPT-assisted drafting/editing visibly at the start of content produced through this workflow, and in the medium's valid metadata tag/topic field. Name other material AI assistance accurately when applicable. Plain disclosure is required even after human revision; style work must improve content, not conceal AI authorship. Check the platform's current syntax: a text hashtag is not automatically a platform topic. Record disclosure presence separately from substantive quality and preserve explicit human merge/publication approval.

Write from the author's supported point of view. Open with the situation, goal and obstacle so the reader knows why the investigation mattered. Explain discoveries, attempts, failures and the reasons for the next step in a readable sequence. Use first person where the author actually acted or reached a judgment; distinguish an agent's observation, a user report and a later interpretation. Never invent experiences, emotions, experiments, dialogue or results. Do not force first person into each paragraph.

Choose a specific title and headings that help readers navigate the actual story. Prefer concrete transitions over a template. Avoid mechanical status-report prose, stock AI phrasing, excessive politeness and a generic moral at the end. A short article needs no ceremonial introduction, table of contents or conclusion. Write naturally in the intended language; an English translation needs its own editorial pass.

Keep observed facts, reported outcomes, hypotheses and opinions distinguishable. Preserve dates, values, command semantics, attribution and limitations. Show enough environment and diagnostic context to reproduce the reasoning; label illustrative commands and separate them from commands actually executed. A small successful example does not prove a general fix. Keep secrets, customer information and private host details out of drafts and review artifacts.

Use the [technical-writing workflow](../skills/technical-writing/SKILL.md). An Editor evaluates readability and voice; a separate Technical Reviewer checks evidence and technical claims. Neither role approves its own work as independent review. Record actual roles, findings and revisions. Deterministic checks support these judgments; counts cannot establish that an article reads well.

A draft PR is review preparation, not approval. Merge and public delivery each require explicit human authorization of the exact article, destination and visibility. Keep unpublished content unpublished. Use [article-editor](../skills/article-editor/SKILL.md) for Draft → Mechanical Lint → Editorial → Technical → Targeted Revision → Final Verification → Ready. Apply the latest specified local stack: Japanese textlint, English Vale, optional local LanguageTool. Use the current identities, measured outcomes and scope in [candidate review](../skills/article-editor/references/candidates.md): noslop is an explicit optional local Japanese adapter, slop-shield contributes communication guidance, and the ambiguous testlint-ja name is not silently substituted. Optional candidates are not mandatory blockers. Never invent execution. Do not send unpublished drafts to external APIs by default.

BLOCKER findings (substantial technical errors, security/privacy issues or clear falsehoods) require resolution. MAJOR findings normally require revision; proceeding needs a recorded reason, remaining impact and reconsideration condition. MINOR/SUGGESTION findings do not stop progress. At most two review/revision cycles; end Ready for human review or Blocked with concrete unresolved evidence. New substantial evidence requires a recorded reopening decision rather than an endless loop. A missing primary tool is a reported MAJOR concern; optional LanguageTool absence is a non-blocking limitation. These severities supersede the earlier blanket unrun-tool hold. Preserve actual statuses, protected-span/meaning review and explicit human merge/publication authorization.
